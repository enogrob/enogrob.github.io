What if an AI system could describe not only what it should do, but what it is trying to understand?

AI agents are rapidly becoming capable of planning, calling tools, delegating work, maintaining state, inspecting results, and iterating until a task reaches a stopping point.

A simplified agent loop looks like this:

That model is extraordinarily useful. But it is primarily organized around action.

There is another question worth exploring:

What would an AI-native architecture look like if understanding itself became a first-class computational object?

Not understanding hidden inside model weights. Not understanding implied by a final paragraph. But understanding that can be structured, traced, questioned, projected, revised, and acted upon.

This is the territory explored by HOLOFLUX, IOP, and COSMOS.

1. From execution to inquiry

A conventional agent can be represented conceptually as:

agent :researcher do
  goal :answer_question

  plan
  select_tool
  execute
  evaluate
  repeat
end

Its central question is:

What should I do next?

An inquiry-oriented system introduces another question before action:

What is actually happening here?

intent :understand do
  observe :what_is

  expose :assumptions
  expose :contradictions

  preserve :unknowns
  preserve :uncertainty

  allow :insight

  act_from :understanding
end

The movement becomes:

Action remains important. It is simply no longer the only organizing principle.

2. The missing epistemic layer

Consider concepts that frequently become mixed together inside AI-generated answers:

Reference
Source
Source Passage
Observation
Claim
Evidence
Hypothesis
Contradiction
Uncertainty
Understanding

They are related, but they are not interchangeable:

Citation ≠ Source
Source ≠ Evidence
Evidence ≠ Claim
Claim ≠ Understanding
Operational Success ≠ Epistemic Acceptance

A model successfully retrieving a document does not establish the claims contained in that document. A source asserting something does not automatically turn the assertion into evidence. A generated explanation does not automatically become understanding.

What if those distinctions became part of the runtime?

3. A practical case: the Grebennikov inquiry

Viktor S. Grebennikov was an entomologist and naturalist whose book My World includes a chapter describing what he called the Cavity Structural Effect (CSE) and extraordinary claims involving antigravitational phenomena.

The purpose of using this case is not to assert that antigravity was demonstrated. It is useful precisely because it forces an inquiry system to keep source, observation, interpretation, evidence, contradiction, and unknowns separate.

A weak summarization pipeline can collapse the semantic layers into something like:

Grebennikov discovered an antigravity effect associated with insect structures.

An inquiry-oriented representation keeps the distinctions visible:

Grebennikov reported observation X.
Grebennikov interpreted X as effect Y.
He proposed mechanism Z.
Independent confirmation is a separate question.
Alternative explanations remain possible.
The claim cannot be treated as established merely because it appears in the primary source.

4. Starting with intent

Instead of beginning with:

task :summarize_grebennikov

we can begin with:

intent :understand_antigravity_claim

A task such as summarize already specifies an output. An intent such as understand defines a purpose while leaving the conclusion open.

intent :understand_antigravity_claim do
  preserve :unknown
  avoid :premature_conclusion
  avoid :confusing_claim_with_evidence
end

The system has not been asked to prove Grebennikov correct or wrong. It has been asked to understand what can actually be concluded.

The reference is now part of the semantic model rather than merely an item in a bibliography.

6. Epistemic policies

Before asking the system to investigate anything, we can describe the rules under which knowledge may be constructed:

policies do
  distinguish :observation, :claim
  distinguish :claim, :evidence
  distinguish :source, :evidence
  distinguish :hypothesis, :demonstrated_mechanism

  preserve :provenance
  preserve :contradiction
  preserve :uncertainty
  preserve :unknown
end

These are not merely security guardrails. They are epistemic guardrails.

policy :claim_acceptance do
  require :supporting_evidence
  preserve :contradictory_evidence
  preserve :source_identity
  prohibit :source_as_evidence_by_default
end

7. HOLOFLUX: making the semantic field explicit

A compact formulation is:

HOLOFLUX = Meaning + Explication

Applied to the Grebennikov case:

holoflux :grebennikov_field do

  source :grebennikov_my_world do
    reference :grebennikov_primary_source
    author "Viktor S. Grebennikov"
    chapter 5
  end

  observation :o1 do
    statement "Certain insect structures were reported as having unusual microscopic geometries."
    provenance do
      source :grebennikov_my_world
      location :chapter_5
    end
  end

  claim :c1 do
    statement "Cavity structures produce a distinct physical effect."
    asserted_by :grebennikov
    provenance do
      source :grebennikov_my_world
      location :chapter_5
    end
  end

  claim :c2 do
    statement "The proposed effect can produce antigravitational behavior."
    asserted_by :grebennikov
    depends_on :c1
    provenance do
      source :grebennikov_my_world
      location :chapter_5
    end
  end

  evidence :e1 do
    supports :c2
    status :insufficient
    provenance do
      source :grebennikov_my_world
      kind :reported_experience
    end
  end

  unknown :u1 do
    asks "Is there an independently reproducible anomalous force?"
  end

  unknown :u2 do
    asks "Can the reported phenomenon be experimentally distinguished from known physical effects?"
  end

  uncertainty :high
end

The prose has become a semantic field that can be inspected without collapsing observation, claim, evidence, unknown, and uncertainty into one statement.

8. IOP: inquiry without a predetermined conclusion

IOP = Intent + Understanding

Intent here does not mean produce answer X. It defines the mode of inquiry.

inquiry :antigravity_claim do
  observe :reported_phenomena
  observe :measurement_quality
  observe :replication_status
  observe :alternative_explanations

  preserve :unknown

  do_not_assume :claim_is_true
  do_not_assume :claim_is_false

  ask "What is actually demonstrated?"
  ask "What remains interpretation?"
  ask "What observation could discriminate between competing explanations?"
end

The conclusion does not need to be forced.

9. allow, not produce 

produce :insight

implies that insight is mechanically generated from a command. An inquiry-oriented DSL can instead say:

emergence :insight do
  conditions :attention,
             :observation,
             :sufficient_context
  commanded false
end

or:

inquiry :understand do
  observe :what_is
  preserve :contradiction
  preserve :unknown
  remain_with :question
  allow :insight
end

This introduces an important distinction:

cause ≠ condition ≠ emergence

10. Understanding is not the output

Suppose the inquiry reaches this state:

The primary source reports an antigravity interpretation of an alleged phenomenon.
The existence of the source establishes that the claim was made.
It does not by itself establish that the proposed physical mechanism is valid.
Independent reproducibility therefore remains a central unresolved question.

That understanding can produce several outputs.

The outputs are projections. They are not the understanding itself.

11. COSMOS as the orchestration layer

COSMOS coordinates the whole:

COSMOS = Orchestration of the Whole


cosmos :grebennikov_inquiry do

  reference :primary_source do
    author "Viktor S. Grebennikov"
    work "My World"
    chapter 5
    title "The Natural Phenomena of AntiGravitation and Invisibility in Insects due to the Grebennikov Cavity Structure Effect"
    source_type :book_chapter
    language :english_translation
    url "https://geomagnetics.org/sitebuildercontent/sitebuilderfiles/myworldbyviktorgrebennikovfinpdf.pdf"
  end

  intent :understand_antigravity_claim do
    preserve :unknown
    avoid :premature_conclusion
    avoid :confusing_claim_with_evidence
  end

  context do
    include reference(:primary_source)
    include :reported_observations
    include :known_physics
    include :independent_sources
    include :replication_history
  end

  policies do
    distinguish :observation, :claim
    distinguish :claim, :evidence
    distinguish :source, :evidence
    distinguish :hypothesis, :demonstrated_mechanism

    preserve :provenance
    preserve :contradiction
    preserve :uncertainty
  end

  capabilities do
    use :retrieval
    use :source_validation
    use :holoflux
    use :iop
    use :diagram_generation
  end

  orchestrate do
    acquire :sources

    delegate :semantic_explication,
             to: :holoflux

    delegate :inquiry,
             to: :iop

    integrate :understanding
  end

  project do
    as :dsl
    as :mermaid
    as :epistemic_report
  end
end

The DSL describes:

Intent
Context
Policies
Capabilities
Orchestration
Understanding
Projection

It describes the epistemic architecture of the system.

13. Implicate and explicate

The architecture suggests an analogy with David Bohm’s distinction between implicate and explicate order. This is a conceptual analogy, not a claim that the runtime implements Bohm’s physical theory.

A text contains more possible meaning than any one representation of it. Relationships, tensions, assumptions, interpretations, perspectives, and unanswered questions remain partially enfolded.

A DSL can then be seen as one explicit projection:

The resulting semantic field can then be projected as a DSL, diagram, narrative, or behavior without confusing any one projection with the whole.

The DSL is therefore not the implicate order itself. It is an explication of part of it.

14. From documentation to executable architecture

At first, this DSL can serve as architectural documentation. The more interesting possibility is that it eventually becomes executable.

cosmos :research_system do
  intent :understand_article

  policies do
    preserve :unknown
    preserve :provenance
  end

  capabilities do
    use :retrieval
    use :holoflux
    use :iop
  end
end

A runtime could resolve use :retrieval into search, RAG, vector storage, an MCP server, a knowledge graph, or an agent. COSMOS deals with capabilities; the runtime deals with implementation.

15. Agent behavior can become part of the DSL

agent :researcher do
  behavior :investigate_claim do
    receive :claim
    retrieve :sources
    preserve :provenance

    distinguish :source,
                :evidence,
                :claim

    detect :contradictions
    assess :uncertainty

    open :inquiry,
         when: :unknown_remains

    emit :understanding
  end
end

17. A Krishnamurti-inspired semantics of inquiry

The language of IOP draws inspiration from concepts often explored by J. Krishnamurti:

observation
conditioning
conflict
attention
ending
insight
intelligence

The purpose is not to turn those concepts into a deterministic algorithm. Instead, they suggest another vocabulary for computational inquiry:

intent :understand do
  not :control
  not :premature_conclusion

  observe :what_is
  preserve :unknown
  remain_with :question

  allow :insight
end

The distinction between produce :insight and allow :insight is intentional. The first describes command. The second describes conditions under which something may emerge.

18. What the Grebennikov example reveals

The antigravity case is useful precisely because it prevents the system from taking an easy path.

An AI could simply say:

Grebennikov discovered antigravity.

or jump to the opposite shortcut:

The claim is false.

An inquiry-oriented architecture should resist both shortcuts when the available semantic field does not justify them.

Its strength is not that it produces a stronger opinion. Its strength is that it preserves the difference between:

known
claimed
supported
contradicted
uncertain
unknown

Perhaps the important transition is not merely:

software → agents

but:

execution → inquiry

and eventually:

opaque reasoning → explicit understanding

Conclusion

AI agents already know how to act. Increasingly, they know how to choose tools, delegate work, maintain context, inspect results, and continue autonomously.

The next architectural question may be different.

Can an AI-native system make explicit:

what was observed
what was claimed
where it came from
what supports it
what contradicts it
what remains uncertain
what remains unknown
what can actually be understood

If so, the fundamental loop changes:

becomes:

The system no longer asks only:

What should I do next?

It can also ask:

What is actually happening?

What do I know?

What don’t I know?

And what do I truly understand?

That is where HOLOFLUX, IOP, and COSMOS begin to converge — not merely into another agent framework, but into an architecture for AI-native understanding.
