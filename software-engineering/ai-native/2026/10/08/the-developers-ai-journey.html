OPEN MIND · INQUIRY · ENGINEERING · XXI → 0

There has never been so much AI within a developer's reach—and perhaps never so much uncertainty about where to begin.

One day we are discovering autocomplete. Soon we are debating prompts, vibe coding, context windows, agentic workflows, MCP, Skills, AI-driven delivery, and AI-native systems. Every week offers another tool, another framework, another claim that everything has changed.

What does a developer actually need to learn? And more importantly, what is changing in the way we understand and build software?

The journey is not about mastering AI. It is about transforming understanding.

A map, not a maturity ladder

The Fool’s Journey, a popular narrative interpretation of the 22 Major Arcana, gives us an intriguing metaphor. The Fool (0) represents openness: the willingness to step forward without possessing every answer. The World (XXI) represents integration: seeing connections that were invisible at the beginning. But the story does not end there. XXI returns to 0. Experience changes the traveler; uncertainty remains.

This is a literary and educational metaphor, not a claim that tarot predicts technological progress. Nor is it a prescription that every developer must encounter every tool in the same order. Different capabilities overlap; they are not badges on a universal career ladder.

The central question

Instead of asking “Which tool comes after prompts?”, ask “What problem am I trying to understand, and what capability would help me investigate it responsibly?”

Three paths through the journey

The Secret of the Sphinx explores a symbolic reading of three paths: Osiris, Thoth–Sopdet, and Horus. We can borrow that interpretive lens for software engineering without suggesting that it is an established historical taxonomy of tarot or a mandated order of technological progress.

These are three interwoven paths with specific Arcana sequences, not three compulsory levels. Transformation challenges assumptions; awakening deepens observation and understanding; experience tests understanding through action. Their symbolic lessons can overlap in practice, but the card sequences shown below retain their defined order.

PATH I

Osiris · Transformation

What must I reconsider or leave behind?

From the assumption that productivity means typing code, to questioning premature trust, obsolete habits, and fragile automation.

Developer practice: review assumptions, recognize failure modes, and redesign the process.

PATH II

Thoth–Sopdet · Awakening

What am I beginning to understand?

Prompts, context, tools, MCP, agents, and Skills expand what can be explored—but do not automatically establish what is true.

Developer practice: investigate, ask better questions, and make uncertainty explicit.

PATH III

Horus · Experience

How can I apply and validate what I understand?

Turn hypotheses into bounded workflows, useful software, tests, reviews, evaluations, and accountable AI-native design.

Developer practice: build, measure, verify, and learn from consequences.

0 · Open mind→ Transformation · Awakening · Experience→ XXI · Integration↻ 0 · Renewed inquiry

Three paths, one evolving understanding. Transformation changes our assumptions. Awakening changes what we notice. Experience tests what we build. None of them removes the need for the others.

The arcana remain the symbolic encounters of the story; these paths describe different ways to interpret what happens during those encounters. For example, the Hanged Man (XII) belongs to the Path of Horus, the Tower (XVI) to the Path of Osiris, and Justice (XI) to the Path of Thoth–Sopdet. These correspondences are editorial interpretations rather than fixed tarot rules.

Act I — The Awakening

Explore Arcana 0–IVOpen mind, code completion, prompts, creativity and workflows
ARCANUM 0

The Fool — An open mind

Imagine a developer in a familiar environment: editor, terminal, tests, pull requests, deadlines. A new assistant appears in the IDE. Curiosity competes with skepticism: Will this help? Will I lose my skills? Should I trust it?

An open mind is not unconditional enthusiasm. It is the willingness to experiment without surrendering judgment. The first change is not in the stack; it is in the posture toward the unknown.

ARCANUM I

The Magician — Code completion

An intelligently suggested line, a function, a test scaffold: the developer still chooses the direction, while AI reduces friction at the keyboard.

The small convenience raises a much larger question: If AI can complete code, what else could it help me do?

ARCANUM II

The High Priestess — Prompts and context

Conversation expands beyond completion: explain an unfamiliar Rails service, surface trade-offs, compare approaches, or challenge an assumption.

Strong prompting depends on context, constraints, examples, and iterative inquiry—not magical wording or presumed correctness.

ARCANUM III

The Empress — Vibe coding and creation

Natural-language generation accelerates prototypes. An idea becomes an interface or small product quickly enough to discover what the developer actually wants.

But vibe coding, especially without serious code inspection, is not the same as production-grade engineering. Demonstrating possibility is not proving reliability.

ARCANUM IV

The Emperor — Workflows and structure

Instead of collecting disconnected answers, turn work into a reproducible sequence: research, design, implement, test, review, publish. Make inputs, checks, and outputs explicit.

The emphasis shifts from one useful answer to a dependable way of working.

WEAK REQUEST

“Refactor this job.”

ENGINEERING REQUEST

“Identify failure modes in this Sidekiq job, preserve idempotency, propose alternatives, and show which assumptions require tests.”

Act II — The Turning Point

Explore Arcana V–XVISkills, tools, agency, verification, perspective and risks
ARCANUM V

The Hierophant — Skills and tradition

Expertise can be captured in reusable Skills: procedures, project conventions, examples, review criteria, and domain knowledge.

The question changes from What should I prompt? to Which practice can my team repeat and inspect?

ARCANUM VI

The Lovers — Human–AI collaboration

AI can propose and execute. The developer still frames intent, defines boundaries, evaluates trade-offs, and accepts responsibility.

Good collaboration is neither total delegation nor refusal to delegate; it is a deliberate division of work.

ARCANUM VII

The Chariot — Tools and MCP

Tools expose concrete actions; MCP provides a protocol for connecting approved capabilities and context. An assistant can reach repositories, APIs, documentation, and tests.

Access expands what can be done, but permissions and provenance must remain explicit.

Tools / MCPAccess to systems and actions
AgentsPlanning and multistep execution
SkillsReusable ways of doing work
VerificationEvidence that results meet expectations
ARCANUM VIII

Strength — Controlled autonomy

Agents can plan and perform multistep work, including editing files, running checks, and proposing a pull request.

Controlled autonomy requires bounded goals, permissions, traceability, checkpoints, and human approval where warranted.

ARCANUM IX

The Hermit — Reflection and discernment

When a result looks convincing, the developer must still ask: How do I know?

Reflection means examining assumptions, reading the code, reviewing generated explanations, and seeking evidence beyond fluent output.

ARCANUM X

Wheel of Fortune — A changing landscape

Models, products, and workflows evolve. What works today may change with the next release or context window.

Keep the engineering principles portable: explicit requirements, reproducible tests, observability, and the ability to recover safely.

ARCANUM XI

Justice — Verification and accountability

A passing test does not necessarily prove the right behavior was tested. A link does not automatically supply evidence. A successful tool call is not a validated business outcome.

Verification joins correctness, security, provenance, and accountable review. Capability is not correctness.

ARCANUM XII

The Hanged Man — A change in perspective

This is the turning point: producing more code faster is not the same as knowing what should be built, why, and how to determine whether it fulfills the intent.

Code becomes an expression of understanding, never its substitute.

ARCANUM XIII

Death — Letting go of assumptions

Some assumptions must be released: that professional value is measured by lines typed, that every task needs an agent, or that a demo proves readiness.

Leaving those assumptions behind makes more room for domain understanding and sound engineering choices.

ARCANUM XIV

Temperance — Human–AI integration

Balance automation with tests, architecture, observability, human communication, and operational constraints.

The goal is not to choose human or machine, but to design a process in which each contributes what can be justified and verified.

ARCANUM XV

The Devil — The dependency trap

Convenience can become dependence: unfamiliar code is accepted, investigation is skipped, and authority is delegated without safeguards.

The danger is less the presence of AI than the disappearance of independent judgment.

ARCANUM XVI

The Tower — Failure reveals the architecture

Reality intervenes: a payment job retries twice, a data migration violates invariants, or an agent changes the wrong files.

Failure asks a sharper question: What did our process fail to understand?

Capability is not correctness.

AI can generate options. Engineering judgment evaluates constraints, evidence, risks, and consequences.

Act III — Integration and Return

Explore Arcana XVII–XXINew perspectives, uncertainty, clarity and AI-native integration
ARCANUM XVII

The Star — Renewed vision

A renewed engineering vision sees relationships among people, knowledge, tools, models, policies, and evaluations.

We can pursue better outcomes rather than simply more generated output.

ARCANUM XVIII

The Moon — Uncertainty and evidence

Models generate claims; systems retrieve sources; neither operation proves understanding. Uncertainty must be represented rather than concealed.

Distinguish output, source, evidence, claim, and understanding, and keep unresolved questions visible.

ARCANUM XIX

The Sun — Clarity through inquiry

Clarity grows when intent is explicit and observations can be tested against expectations.

The objective is not certainty about everything. It is an honest, revisable account of what the system does and why.

ARCANUM XX

Judgement — Reimagining architecture

The architectural question emerges: when should AI participate in a running product rather than only in the development workflow?

Think about model inference, tools, context, evaluation, governed actions, and feedback as designed capabilities—not magic defaults.

ARCANUM XXI

The World — Integration, not completion

A wider picture comes into view. Prompts, Skills, agents, workflows, and models become design choices in service of people and real constraints.

The World is integration, not final mastery. From XXI the developer returns to 0—with fresh questions and a wider perspective.

DEVELOPMENT ASSISTANCE

AI-Assisted

AI helps developers build.

Example: GitHub Copilot suggests Rails code, explains a service, or drafts a test for human review.

ENGINEERING WORKFLOW

AI-Driven

AI drives bounded parts of the process.

Example: An agent proposes a change, runs tests, and prepares a pull request for human approval.

PRODUCT ARCHITECTURE

AI-Native

AI participates in the running system.

Example: A product integrates model inference, tools, context, evaluation, and governed actions.

These are working distinctions, not an official certification ladder. An AI-native product can still be developed with traditional tooling; an AI-assisted team can ship entirely deterministic software.

A practical engineering example

Suppose you are asked to improve the reliability of payment processing in a Rails application.

An autocomplete-only approach helps write another retry method. A prompt-first approach asks for recommendations. A workflow-driven approach inspects the code, develops a failure model, implements changes, runs checks, and requires review. With tools and agents, much of that work can be coordinated across repository, CI, and documentation. With Skills, project-specific conventions can become repeatable.

But none of those capabilities can replace the critical questions: Is the operation idempotent? What happens under duplicate delivery? Which side effects can be reversed? Which invariant proves business correctness? Who approves the change?

OUTPUT-CENTRIC

“The AI wrote a retry policy.”

UNDERSTANDING-CENTRIC

“We identified the failure modes, tested the invariants, and can explain why the recovery policy is appropriate.”

The deepest gain is not the number of generated lines. It is the ability to discover, test, and preserve a more accurate account of the system.

Where are you in the journey?

Do not measure your progress by how many new products you have installed. Ask which questions you can now answer:

  • Exploring? Can you explain what you want AI to help you discover?
  • Prompting? Can you supply context, constraints, and verification criteria?
  • Automating? Can your workflow fail visibly and recover safely?
  • Connecting tools? Can you describe what the system is permitted to access and change?
  • Delegating to agents? Can you tell the difference between execution success and task correctness?
  • Designing AI-native systems? Can you represent uncertainty, audit decisions, and evaluate outcomes?
Compass, not checklist

Find your current question. Choose the smallest capability that helps answer it. Verify what you learned. Repeat.

XXI → 0: the return

The Fool begins without knowing. The World arrives with a wider understanding. But wisdom is not the absence of uncertainty. It is the ability to return to uncertainty with better questions, stronger methods, and greater responsibility.

The engineer who first discovered intelligent autocomplete is not quite the same engineer who now considers agent orchestration, Skills, evidence, or AI-native architecture. Yet the most important capability remains recognizable: an open mind prepared to investigate what is actually happening.

Every new tool expands what a developer can do. Every new understanding transforms what a developer can become.

The World is not the end of the journey. It is the moment the Fool discovers a new beginning.

The Journey as a Semantic Model

The twenty-two Arcana can be described once as a set of contextual meanings, then related through three interwoven paths. In this interpretation, Context situates each card in AI engineering, Light expresses its constructive possibility, Shadow explores its limitations, and Insight captures a question or understanding that may emerge. These are interpretive perspectives, not proven properties of the cards or evidence about AI systems.

  understanding :evolving do

The semantic definitions and the three paths are distinct: the paths reference the Arcana rather than duplicating them. The return preserves evolving understanding without suggesting that uncertainty has disappeared.

The journey does not repeat itself. It unfolds again from a different understanding.
